Individual Psychotherapy

Individual psychotherapy, also known as talk therapy, counseling, or psychotherapy, is a one-on-one mental health treatment where a trained professional helps an individual explore and address personal issues, emotional difficulties, or mental health concerns. It is a collaborative and confidential process designed to improve well-being by increasing self-awareness, understanding thought and behavior patterns, and developing effective coping strategies for challenges such as anxiety, depression, trauma, relationship issues, stress, and addiction. Therapists use evidence-based techniques like cognitive-behavioral therapy (CBT), psychodynamic therapy, or mindfulness, tailored to the individual’s unique needs and goals. Sessions are typically held regularly, either in person or via teletherapy, and can be short-term for specific concerns or long-term for more complex or chronic conditions

Couples Therapy

Couples therapy is a form of psychotherapy that helps romantic partners improve communication, resolve conflicts, and strengthen their emotional connection.

Therapist Supervision

Therapist supervision is a formal, relationship-based educational process where a licensed or experienced mental health professional guides a trainee or less experienced therapist to develop clinical competence, ethical practice, and professional identity. It serves as the signature pedagogy of the mental health professions, playing a crucial role in the development of psychotherapist competence and practice excellence. Supervision is required for all mental health providers seeking licensure, with specific hour and supervision requirements varying by state and license type.
